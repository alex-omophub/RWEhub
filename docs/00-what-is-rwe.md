# 00 · What is Real-World Evidence?

## From data to evidence

- **Real-World Data (RWD)** — health data routinely collected during care or
  daily life, *not* generated by a controlled experiment.
- **Real-World Evidence (RWE)** — the clinical conclusions about a treatment's
  use, benefit, or risk that you *derive by analyzing RWD*.

RWD is the raw material; RWE is the finished, decision-grade product. The gap
between them is filled by data engineering, study design, and statistics — the
subject of this hub.

## Common RWD sources

| Source | What it contains | Typical use |
|--------|------------------|-------------|
| **EHR** (electronic health records) | Diagnoses, labs, vitals, notes | Effectiveness, disease natural history |
| **Administrative claims** | Billing codes, procedures, dispensed drugs | Utilization, adherence, safety at scale |
| **Disease/product registries** | Curated, condition-specific cohorts | Long-term outcomes, rare disease |
| **Patient-reported outcomes (PRO)** | Symptoms, quality of life | Patient-centered endpoints |
| **Wearables / digital** | Continuous physiologic signals | Novel endpoints, remote monitoring |

## Why standardization matters: the OMOP CDM

Every EHR and claims database is structured differently. The **OMOP Common Data
Model (CDM)**, stewarded by the [OHDSI](https://www.ohdsi.org/) community,
transforms any source into a shared schema with standardized vocabularies
(SNOMED, RxNorm, LOINC). Benefits:

- **Portability** — the same analysis code runs on any OMOP database worldwide.
- **Reproducibility** — network studies can be replicated across sites without
  sharing patient-level data.
- **Tooling** — the OHDSI toolstack (ATLAS, Achilles, HADES) works out of the box.

> 🔑 If you learn one infrastructure concept as an RWE intern, make it the OMOP CDM.

## What makes RWE *credible*

Because there's no randomization, RWE studies must actively defend against bias:

1. **Confounding** — patients on treatment A differ from those on B. Handled
   with propensity scores, matching, IPTW, and adjustment.
2. **Selection bias** — who enters the data and when (e.g., immortal-time bias).
3. **Measurement/misclassification** — codes are proxies for truth.
4. **Missing data** — rarely missing at random in health records.

The **target trial emulation** framework (design your RWE study as if you were
emulating the randomized trial you *wish* you could run) is the current
gold-standard mental model.

## The regulatory backdrop

- **FDA** — the 21st Century Cures Act (2016) directed FDA to evaluate RWE for
  regulatory decisions; multiple guidance documents now cover RWD quality and
  study design.
- **EMA** — the DARWIN EU network operationalizes RWE across Europe.

RWE now supports label expansions, single-arm trial external controls, and
post-marketing safety commitments.
